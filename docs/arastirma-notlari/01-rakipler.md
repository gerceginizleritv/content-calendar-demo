## 2. Group notes (what matters beyond the table)

**Group 1, schedulers.** All 22 auto-publish; none has a "shoot/project → many posts" entity. The nearest constructs are Buffer's Ideas boards (kanban, then "add to queue"), Planable's sync toggle (one draft → per-platform variants), Loomly's per-post workflow states, and ContentStudio/Kontentino's campaign/kanban views. All price on channels, social sets, seats or workspaces, which is exactly what solo creators complain about (see D). Instagram-first planners (Planoly, Plann, Preview) are grid planners; only Preview offers unlimited planning free.

**Group 2, planning/approval tools.** Planable, Kontentino, Gain and HeyOrca all publish; "approval-first" does not mean "non-publishing". Planable's free plan (50 posts, ever) is the only free planning-first tier with unlimited users. Price floors: Planable $33/workspace, HeyOrca $59/calendar, Kontentino €49–69, Gain $99. Nothing here targets a solo video creator's production process.

**Group 3, general tools and the template economy.**
- Notion: Free / Plus $10 / Business $20 per user (annual). AI now sits in Business; agent credits are metered since May 2026.
- Gumroad content-calendar Notion templates seen in search: $1.70 (nupurverse), $7 (invinciblecareer), $10 (henrikolofsson "Content Planner", "focus on simplicity"), $24.49 (Notion Everything "Content Planner & Calendar", 10 preconfigured channels, idea hub, pipeline kanban); several free (Marketing Fairy, Mr Wolf's Workshop lite, "Simple Content Planner"). SendOwl's guide puts multi-page content-calendar systems at $10–19.
- Premium creator systems: Thomas Frank's Creator's Companion (idea → research → script → editorial calendar → performance → brand deals) at $149 Base to $199; his Notion template business crossed ~$2.1M in about two years, with the Creator's Companion/Ultimate Brain bundle at ~$298k, CC Ultimate Tasks ~$86k and CC Base ~$17k (creator-reported figures, not audited). His "Notion Video Project Tracker" for YouTubers is free. Easlo reported $239k in 2022 template sales; one Easlo template shows 979 buyers at $79. (All self-reported.)
- Etsy: content-calendar templates are mostly Google Sheets/Excel/Notion at roughly $8–25; one listing shows 4.8 stars with 716 reviews; 2026-dated Sheets planners with 4.8+ ratings and "Star Seller" badges exist. Etsy does not display sales counts, so volume is inferable only from review counts.
- Notion Marketplace: most content planners listed are free (e.g., Núria Gabàs' Content Creator Planner, "Content Planner" 4.65 from 10 ratings); paid prices were not visible in search results.
- Creative Market: TinyPineCreative 98-page Canva content calendar; Marketello Studio Google Sheets marketing calendar; prices not retrievable.
- Milanote sells $9.99/mo boards with YouTube video-plan, channel-plan, schedule and script templates. Stackby and Kanbanchi market video-production tracker templates. Frame.io is review, not planning.

**Group 4, YouTube-specific.** TubeBuddy and vidIQ are SEO/AI-idea tools priced $3–49/mo; vidIQ's Daily Ideas produces 50 ideas/day and AI titles/scripts but neither has a production calendar (TubeBuddy could not be re-verified). Dedicated pipeline apps exist but are small: Storyflow ($7.99/mo, idea-to-script boards), Peliku (ideas/scripts/shots/publishing for teams; pricing unknown), VidStack (kanban idea → published plus credit-based AI and a freelancer marketplace), OverseerOS ($29+, AI strategy with a planner), TubeMator (unverified). AFFiNE and Thomas Frank give away YouTube planner templates. None found combines shoot/location/permit fields with per-platform post fan-out and time zones.

**Group 5, open source.** Postiz (AGPL-3.0, 35.4k stars, weekly releases, hosted from $29/mo or free self-host) and Mixpost (MIT Lite, paid Pro $299 one-time) are the credible ones; Socioboard is effectively dormant. All are publishers; a self-hoster needs servers plus platform developer apps, and the AI features need the user's own OpenAI key. Planning-only OSS repos on the "content-planner" topic have 0–3 stars. No open-source project undercuts a planning-only PWA because none exists with traction; the OSS threat is only to the publishing side, which Slate does not have.

**Group 6, AI-first.** Predis, Ocoya, ContentStudio bundle credit-metered AI into schedulers ($15–32 entry). Lately hides pricing ($49–199 reported). Jasper ($59–69) and Copy.ai ($24–29 entry, $249+ for agents) are writing tools, not calendars. BYOK exists at the edges: SocialAI360 markets it explicitly; Postiz and Mixpost use the operator's own OpenAI key; Postiz users are asking for OpenAI-compatible/local endpoints (issue #1074, open). Separately, Ayrshare's SDK now requires users to bring their own X developer keys (from 31 Mar 2026), and Mixpost's latest release exists to support "the X pay-as-you-go tier": platform API costs are being pushed to end users.

## A. Is there a gap for a planning-only pipeline tool with BYOK AI?

Yes, in the sense that no product found matches the concept. No, in the sense that the empty space is partly explained by weak willingness to pay for planning alone.

Closest existing competitors, and how they differ:
1. Thomas Frank's Creator's Companion (Notion, $149–199 one-time). Same chain (ideas → script → production → calendar), YouTube-native, proven demand. Differs: requires Notion, no per-platform time-slot/time-zone fan-out, no location/permit fields, AI costs extra via Notion Business ($20/user), heavy to maintain.
2. Peliku. Same "idea to publication" production framing with scripts and shot lists. Differs: team/agency orientation, unknown price, appears cloud-only; multi-platform post fan-out not evidenced.
3. VidStack. Kanban idea → published for YouTube, plus AI. Differs: AI is credit-metered and bundled with a freelancer marketplace; no per-platform social calendar.
4. Storyflow ($7.99/mo). Cheapest planning-only creator tool; idea → script. Differs: stops at the script; no calendar, no platforms, no shoot fields.
5. Buffer Ideas + free plan. Free kanban idea board that feeds a queue, with per-channel customization. Differs: publishes, per-channel pricing beyond 3 channels, no project entity, no shoot fields, AI is Buffer's not yours.
6. Planable free (50 posts) / $33. Best "one draft → many platforms with edits" model, approval-first. Differs: per-workspace pricing, post cap, no project/shoot layer, publishes.
7. Milanote YouTube templates ($9.99/mo) and Notion/Trello/Sheets DIY. Same job, zero structure; you build the fan-out yourself.

What none of them do together: local-first/no-account operation, explicit shoot/location/permit/field-notes production layer, one project fanning out to platform-specific post types with time zones, BYOK Gemini, and bilingual TR/EN. The strongest supporting evidence for a non-publishing tool is structural: Crowdfire closed (15 May 2025) citing better native tools and rising API costs; Later dropped X mid-subscription; Metricool doubled its X add-on; Ayrshare and Mixpost are pushing X API costs to users; Instagram gave every public account native scheduling in March 2026. Publishing integrations are now a cost and churn liability that a planning-only tool sidesteps.

The skeptical counterweight: the market prices "planning" between $0 and ~$25 one-time (templates), or as a feature inside a $5–30/mo publisher; the only expensive planning products are Notion systems sold on a big creator's audience. BYOK also assumes users will create a Gemini API key, which mainstream creators may not do; the observed BYOK demand is from self-hosters and developers (Postiz issue, BYOKList), not from Instagram creators.

## B. Price bands

- Free tiers are common and meaningful: Buffer (3 channels/10 posts), Metricool (1 brand/~20 posts), Publer, Vista Social (3 profiles), Pallyy and Typefully (15 posts), Tailwind (5 posts), Planable (50 posts total), HeyOrca Solo (15 posts), Predis (15 AI posts), Plann (IG-only), Preview (unlimited planning), vidIQ, Notion, Milanote, Frame.io; Postiz/Mixpost Lite free to self-host; Meta Business Suite and Instagram native at $0.
- Entry paid tier for one solo creator: $5–6 per channel (Buffer), $5 per account (Publer), $8 per social set (Typefully), $15 (Ocoya, Pallyy, Plann), $16–19 (Metricool, ContentStudio annual, FeedHive), $25 (Later), $28 (Planoly), $29 (SocialBee, Sendible, Hypefury, Postiz hosted, OverseerOS), $33 (Planable), $39 (Vista Social), $59 (Sked, HeyOrca), €49–69 (Kontentino), $79–99 (Agorapulse, Hootsuite, Gain), $199+ (Sprout).
- Creator-niche tools: TubeBuddy ~$3–23/mo annual, vidIQ ~$16.58–39/mo annual, Storyflow $7.99, Milanote $9.99, Frame.io $15.
- Annual discounts cluster at 15–25%, with outliers to 34% (ContentStudio) and ~50% (TubeBuddy).
- Lifetime/one-time: Mixpost Pro $299; AppSumo lifetime deals for schedulers at $59–69 (Sociamonials $69, 4.7 stars from 230+ reviews; Ocoya's from-$69 deal sold out); templates $7–25 typical, $149–199 for premium creator systems.
- Cheapest credible competitors for the same job: Meta Business Suite / Instagram native ($0), Preview (free unlimited grid planning), Buffer free (3 channels), Planable free (50 posts), Notion free plus a $0–25 template, Storyflow $7.99, Publer $5/account, Postiz self-hosted.

Implication (not a recommendation): a planning-only tool is benchmarked against $0 natives and $10–25 templates, so the defensible bands look like free-forever local mode plus either a one-time payment in the $19–49 template range or a low sync subscription ($3–8/mo), rather than the $15–29/mo scheduler band.

## C. Shutdowns, pivots, price changes 2024–2026

- Crowdfire shut down 15 May 2025 (data deleted 30 Jun 2025, refunds issued), citing declining demand as native platform tools improved plus infrastructure and API-integration costs.
- Later: free plan removed in 2024; four tiers collapsed to three; X support ended 28 Aug 2025; $250M Mavely acquisition (Jan 2025) pivots the company toward creator commerce. Trustpilot fell to 1.3.
- Buffer: Nov 2025 repricing to two per-channel plans, Agency plan retired; free plan gained AI Assistant and inbox; 2026 added a public API and MCP server.
- Hootsuite: free plan removed Mar 2023; entry price from $5.99 to $99, now per seat.
- Metricool: X add-on doubled to $10/mo for new activations, 13 Jul 2026.
- Planoly: $16 Starter withdrawn for new customers (~May 2026); floor now $28.
- Hypefury: $19 at launch to $29–199; Loomly restructured tiers in 2025; Sprout Standard listed at $199 annual/$249 monthly.
- Notion: AI folded into Business ($20/user); metered agent credits from May 2026.
- Ayrshare requires bring-your-own X developer keys from 31 Mar 2026; Mixpost 2.6.0 exists to support X's pay-as-you-go API tier.
- Instagram: native scheduling and insights for all public accounts from 1 Mar 2026; Meta Business Suite plans 75 days out.
- Lately: no public pricing (demo/waitlist), i.e., moved upmarket. Ocoya's lifetime deal is gone; Sociamonials still sells a $69 LTD.
- Not found: any evidence of Tailwind, Planable, Pallyy, Sked or Preview shutting down or pivoting (searches ran out before a Tailwind-specific check).

What it tells us: publishing tools are squeezed between rising platform API costs and free native schedulers, so they push costs onto users (per-channel, per-seat, add-ons) and cut features mid-subscription, generating the billing anger seen on Trustpilot. Planning value is being commoditized by natives and templates; the surviving premium is in workflow structure (Frank's $149–199 system) and in avoiding platform dependencies.

## D. What reviewers praise and complain about

- Buffer (G2 4.3/1,023; Trustpilot 2.1/93): praised for a clean interface and a simple scheduling calendar; complaints are per-channel costs "scaling painfully" past ~5 accounts, thin analytics, failed/duplicate posts and slow support. Reddit threads cited by review aggregators name per-channel pricing as the number-one reason to leave.
- Later (G2 4.5; Trustpilot 1.3/335): praised as a visual Instagram planner; dominant complaints are surprise renewals, charges after cancellation, no refunds, X removal "half-way through an annual subscription", app glitches, weak team features, and the vanished free plan ("used to be free, now $25").
- Metricool (G2 4.5/83; Capterra 4.8/115; Trustpilot 3.9–4.2/519–619): praised for value and analytics; complaints are billing after cancellation, slow support, video-scheduling limits, add-on costs for X/LinkedIn, and a dashboard that "feels cluttered or overwhelming" with multiple brands.
- Planable (G2 4.6/937; Trustpilot 4.5): praised for collaboration and being cheaper than Hootsuite/Sprout/Buffer; complaints are per-workspace pricing escalating for freelancers and small agencies, analytics as a paid add-on, and shallow scheduling/analytics.
- Hootsuite: recurring "auto-renewal traps" and $99 floor; Trustpilot ~1.4 (unverified third-party figure).
- Notion content-calendar templates: sales pages are glowing; independent commentary says calendars "become way too complicated" and get abandoned, notes scatter across pages, and some creators replaced Notion plus a caption app plus a scheduler with a single Google Sheet. Template sellers now explicitly market "simple", "lite" and "without stress" variants, which is indirect evidence that the "I just want a simple calendar" segment is real.
- Common thread across all five: people praise simplicity and a visual calendar; they leave over money mechanics (per-channel, per-workspace, renewals, add-ons, removed features) rather than missing features. No reviewer quote was found asking specifically for a shoot-to-posts model; that need is only visible in the existence of Creator's Companion, Peliku, VidStack and the many "YouTube ops" Notion templates on Gumroad.
