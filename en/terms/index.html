2. What Shootboard is — and is not

Shootboard is a planning board for content. It keeps track of the posts that come out of a shoot: what each one is, when it goes out, and how far along it is.

Publishing is opt-in, and narrow. Shootboard can send a post at a time you set — but only if you connect your own account for that platform, only for the entries where you switch it on yourself, and only once the small uploader on your computer has attached the video file. It is off unless you turn it on, entry by entry. What "sending" means is not the same everywhere:

  • Instagram and Facebook — the post is published. It is live.
  • TikTok — the video is left in your TikTok drafts and you press share yourself. Shootboard does not publish on TikTok. The caption does not travel either: that endpoint carries no text, so you write it in the app.
  • YouTube — the Short is uploaded with its title, description and tags. It arrives as private unless that default has been changed.
  • Threads — the post goes out with your text, and with the cover image if the entry has one. A poll goes out with up to four options; the reply you wrote is posted under it at the time you set.
  • Everywhere else — X, LinkedIn, Pinterest and the rest — posting is still something you do yourself.

When a scheduled post does go out, the video or image and the text you wrote leave Shootboard and go to that platform. That is the point of the feature, but it is worth saying plainly: what you scheduled is what gets published, at the time you set, without asking you again. If it fails, the entry is marked failed and you get an email — Shootboard does not quietly retry forever.

Connecting a platform means accepting its rules too. Each connection is separate and you make it yourself. For YouTube in particular, Shootboard uses YouTube API Services: by connecting a channel you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of your data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. You can revoke that access at any time from myaccount.google.com/permissions or from Shootboard's own YouTube page. What we ask for, store and send is listed in section 7b of the Privacy Policy.

9. Deleting your account

Open My account in the app and use Delete your account. Your calendar, projects, ideas, scripts, places, saved accounts, any API keys and your settings are removed from the server at once, together with your files: the calendar-subscription file, any share link and your automatic backups. It cannot be undone, so export first if you want a copy. The automatic backups are deleted with everything else; they only ever held the last seven days.

One exception, stated plainly. If you used scheduled publishing, the video and cover files were uploaded to separate object storage so that Meta could fetch them from a public address — that is the only way Meta will accept a file. Published files are removed on their own: the tool that uploaded a video deletes it — and its cover — from that storage once every entry using it has gone out. A file that never gets published is dropped by a ninety-day cleanup rule on the storage itself. Deleting your account does not trigger either one, because the app holds no key to that storage; ask us from Feedback if you want something removed sooner.
